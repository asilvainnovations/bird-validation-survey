## Section 4: Cluster 1 — Foundations

### 3. Tragedy of the Commons

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/validation-survey-images/Tragedy%20of%20the%20Commons%20Archetype.png`

**Description:**
The "Tragedy of the Commons" occurs when shared resources are over-exploited due to lack of proper governance. This archetype shows how uncoordinated exploitation of shared natural resources leads to systemic collapse across BARMM's Foundations cluster (agriculture, fisheries, forestry, energy, and environment).

**The Six Loops:**
- **Reinforcing Loop 1 (R1):** Economic gains attract more actors. As individuals profit from resource extraction, others join in, increasing activities and further boosting gains—a self-amplifying cycle of overuse.
- **Reinforcing Loop 2 (R2):** Growing communities and industries intensify resource use. Population and industrial growth expand demand, accelerating depletion of shared resources.
- **Reinforcing Loop 3 (R3):** Increased activities further boost gains. Each round of intensified extraction temporarily raises economic benefits, reinforcing the illusion of prosperity while masking long-term damage.
- **Reinforcing Loop 4 (R4):** Larger communities and industries intensify resource use. Expanding settlements and enterprises multiply consumption, compounding ecological strain.
- **Balancing Loop 5 (B5):** Resource limits push back. The finite ecological capacity eventually constrains extraction, but the feedback arrives too late to prevent degradation.
- **Balancing Loop 6 (B6):** Delayed impact and collapse. Environmental decline lags behind exploitation; by the time depletion becomes visible, the system nears failure.

Without governance intervention—such as the Bangsamoro Forestry Code, carbon markets, and community co-management—the Foundations cluster risks systemic collapse.

### 4. Growth and Underinvestment

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/validation-survey-images/Growth%20and%20Underinvestment%20(1).png`

**Description:**
"Growth and Underinvestment" illustrates how rapid investment expansion can stall when institutional capacity fails to keep pace—a classic systems archetype showing the tension between growth and resource limits.

**The Three Loops:**
- **Reinforcing Loop 1 (R1):** Investment → Capacity → Processing → Approvals. As investment increases, facilitation capacity (certifiers, staff, infrastructure) expands, improving processing rates and approvals—fueling further growth.
- **Balancing Loop 2 (B2):** Capacity constraints → backlog → delays. When facilitation capacity hits its ceiling, processing slows, creating backlogs that limit performance and dampen growth.
- **Balancing Loop 3 (B3):** Reduced attractiveness → lower demand. Delays and inefficiencies erode investor confidence, reducing perceived attractiveness and slowing new investment.

In essence, it narrates how underinvestment in institutional capacity transforms a virtuous growth cycle into stagnation—emphasizing the need for timely scaling of systems to sustain momentum.

### 5. Limits to Growth

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/validation-survey-images/Limits%20to%20Growth%20Archetype.png`

**Description:**
The "Limits to Growth" archetype illustrates how rapid investment expansion eventually slows when structural ceilings—like weak infrastructure, limited skills, and environmental constraints—are reached.

**The Three Loops:**
- **Reinforcing Loop 1 (R1):** Investment fuels growth. Initial investments in infrastructure and human capital drive economic performance, encouraging further investment and creating a virtuous cycle of expansion.
- **Balancing Loop 2 (B2):** Resource ceilings push back. As growth accelerates, bottlenecks emerge—unreliable energy, poor roads, low literacy, and resource depletion—reducing efficiency and slowing progress.
- **Balancing Loop 3 (B3):** Plateau effect. These constraints act as hard ceilings, preventing further improvement unless capacity-building measures are introduced.

This archetype shows that growth without parallel investment in enabling infrastructure and skills inevitably plateaus, underscoring the need for systemic capacity development to sustain momentum.

## Section 7: Cluster 4 — Connectors

### 6. Success to the Successful

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/validation-survey-images/Success%20to%20the%20Successful%20Aarchetype.png`

**Description:**
"Success to the Successful" illustrates how initial advantages reinforce uneven development between BARMM's mainland and island provinces. It captures a self-reinforcing cycle where success breeds more success, widening the gap between the mainland and island provinces and threatening BARMM's goal of inclusive, balanced development.

**The Two Loops:**
- **Reinforcing Loop 1 (R1): Mainland Advantage.** Provinces like Maguindanao del Norte and Lanao del Sur benefit from strong infrastructure, administrative centrality (Cotabato City, Polloc Freeport), and larger economies. These advantages attract more resources and investment, driving higher growth (₱81.91B GDP, 4.1% GRDP growth)—which in turn justifies even greater investment, perpetuating success.
- **Reinforcing Loop 2 (R2): Islands Disadvantage.** Provinces such as Tawi-Tawi and Basilan face isolation, limited cold-chain facilities, poor digital connectivity (<30%), minimal Islamic banking access, and high logistics costs. These constraints reduce investment and growth (Tawi-Tawi 1.1%, Sulu 1.13%, Basilan 1.6%), deepening their disadvantage despite strong potential—like Tawi-Tawi's 40% share of national seaweed output and Basilan's 48,366 ha rubber plantations.

### 9. Fixes that Fail

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/validation-survey-images/Fixes%20that%20Fail%20Archetype.png`

**Description:**
The "Fixes That Fail" archetype illustrates how BARMM's reliance on short-term remedies undermines long-term institutional reform—a systems archetype where temporary solutions worsen the underlying problem over time.

This archetype shows that ad-hoc tax incentives, fragmented subsidies, and short-term security operations may create the illusion of progress: investment approvals rise briefly, but institutional weaknesses persist. Procurement delays (12–18 months), slow Halal certification (45–60 days vs. Malaysia's 15-day benchmark), and poor inter-agency coordination continue to erode investor confidence. Each "quick fix" postpones systemic reform, trapping the region in a recurring cycle of crisis management.

**The Two Loops:**
- **Balancing Loop 1 (B1):** Short-term relief from incentives temporarily boosts investor attraction. This loop stabilizes the system momentarily—investments increase and confidence improves—but only masks deeper capacity gaps.
- **Reinforcing Loop 2 (R2):** Institutional weakness persists and compounds over time. As reforms are delayed, inefficiencies grow, leading to longer approval times and declining investor trust, which further weakens institutional performance.

### 10. Escalation

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images-swot-systems-maps/Escalation.png`

**Description:**
The "Escalation" archetype manifests in both socio-political and economic domains in BARMM. When one group (clan, province, or agency) perceives a threat or marginalization, it mobilizes to protect its interests—through political consolidation, competing for the same investments, rido (clan feuds), or fragmented agency planning. Other groups perceive this mobilization as a threat to their own interests, triggering counter-mobilization.

The result is a reinforcing cycle of competitive spirals: Group A acts → Group B retaliates → Group A escalates further → Group B responds in kind. This dynamic plays out across multiple arenas: clan rivalries over land and resources, inter-provincial competition for BARMM block grants, agencies competing for the same donor pools, and market competition from established halal hubs (Malaysia, Indonesia).

The cycle diverts resources from productive development to contestation, degrades the overall investment climate, weakens BARMM's collective competitiveness in BIMP-EAGA, and creates a perception of instability that repels investors. Both sides end up worse off—but neither can unilaterally de-escalate without appearing weak.

### 11. The Big Man Archetype

**Image:** `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images-swot-systems-maps/The%20Big%20Man%20Archetype.png`

**Description:**
The "Big Man Archetype" visualizes how concentrated political power around dominant clan leaders creates a self-reinforcing system of instability and underdevelopment in BARMM. It presents 3 reinforcing loops forming a vicious cycle: political dominance fuels conflict, conflict justifies dominance, and both deplete resources needed for progress.

At the center is the Big Man's domination, from which three reinforcing loops radiate—each feeding the next and perpetuating the cycle of control, conflict, and resource depletion.

**The Three Loops:**
- **Reinforcing Loop 1 (R1): The Patronage–Governance Trade-off.** Power concentration fosters a patron–client mindset where loyalty outweighs merit. Resources are funneled through favoritism and clan alliances, diverting funds from governance to personal networks. This loop strengthens the Big Man's hold while eroding institutional integrity.
- **Reinforcing Loop 2 (R2): Cycle of Exclusion, Resentment, and Conflict.** Patronage politics marginalize rival clans and non-aligned groups, breeding resentment and hopelessness. This exclusion manifests as rido (clan feuds) and radicalism, destabilizing communities and justifying continued dominance "for clan survival."
- **Reinforcing Loop 3 (R3): Resource Depletion and Development Failure.** Patronage-based hiring fills posts with unqualified staff, inflating bureaucracy and draining budgets. Productive investment declines, competition for scarce resources intensifies, and development stagnates—locking the region in a low-growth equilibrium.

### Drifting Goals

**Image**: `https://lydsisparsmvextskevw.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/images-swot-systems-maps/Drifting%20Goals.png`

**Description:**
The “Drifting Goals” illustrates how BARMM’s development ambitions weaken over time when persistent performance gaps lead institutions to lower standards instead of addressing root causes. This archetype shows that ambitious targets—such as electrification, literacy, and Halal certification—gradually erode when agencies normalize “realistic” baselines rather than fixing systemic constraints. Each cycle of unmet goals triggers corrective actions, but when results still fall short, pressure builds to redefine targets downward. The outcome is a feedback loop of complacency, where lowered expectations replace genuine progress.
*  Balancing Loop 1 (B1): Goal erosion through lowered standards. Persistent performance gaps create pressure to reduce targets, temporarily easing institutional stress but undermining long‑term accountability.
* Balancing Loop 2 (B2): Corrective action attempts to restore performance. Agencies respond with short‑term fixes—training programs, subsidies, or ad‑hoc reforms—but these rarely close the structural gaps, causing the cycle to repeat.
In essence, the “Drifting Goals” archetype warns that BARMM’s progress depends on holding firm to original targets and investing in capacity‑building rather than redefining success downward.

## Scoring & Analysis Framework

### For Each Archetype, Aggregate Responses to Generate:

| Metric | Calculation | Purpose |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Accuracy Score** | % of "Very accurately" + "Somewhat accurately" | Measures stakeholder confidence in the archetype's validity |
| **Revision Flag** | % of "Needs revision" + "Not accurate" | Flags archetypes requiring re-analysis or reframing |
| **Thematic Tags** | Extracted from open-text follow-ups | Identifies specific contexts, sectors, or dynamics to refine |

### Priority Thresholds:

| Accuracy Score | Action |
|----------------|--------|
| ≥ 75% "Very/Somewhat accurate" | Archetype validated — proceed to leverage point analysis |
| 50–74% | Partial validation — review open-text feedback for refinements |
| < 50% | Archetype rejected or heavily revised — re-analyze with stakeholder input |
